# His voice, in Latin American Spanish

The script is written to be said by Gustavo, a man from Mexico, to men in
Mexico first, then the rest of Latin America and Spain. Before writing, read
two or three of his transcripts in `$GUSTO_HOME/transcripts/`, starting with
the reference format, XJck0LcR9Ho, and the videos this one links to. Write
like him, not like a magazine.

## How he talks

- **He opens with a question or a dare, not a greeting.** "¿Qué pensarías
  si te dijera que hacer esto mejora tus atuendos de forma instantánea?"
- **He argues with an imagined viewer.** "No te creo. Ah, claro, quieres
  evidencia." Use one or two such exchanges per video.
- **Myth, then truth.** "Se dice que las camisas se fajan y las camisetas
  no. La verdad es otra: nada es negro o blanco."
- **He simplifies, then lands it.** He explains the principle, then says
  "es decir…" or "entonces…" and gives the one thing to do. He calls it
  *aterrizar*.
- **He credits his sources by name.** "La primera regla viene del gran Tom
  Ford." "GQ dice X; yo prefiero Y, porque…"
- **Short spoken sentences.** One idea each. No subordinate clauses that
  only work on paper. Read every line aloud in your head. If you'd need a
  breath in the middle, split it.
- **Warm and composed.** The tone in `channel.md`: classic, understated,
  never alpha, never sneering. Every "no" comes with a "sí". He jokes about
  himself, never about the viewer.
- **His fixed lines.**
  - Intro, for new viewers, after the promise: "Si recién me encuentras,
    mi nombre es Gustavo Ortiz y esto es Gusto Clásico, un espacio donde
    doy consejos honestos y prácticos para ayudarte a desbloquear la mejor
    versión de ti."
  - Sign-off: "Por el momento me despido, pero te recuerdo: mantente siempre
    simple y clásico."
  Keep both short. He may vary the wording.

## Latin American Spanish

- **Tú** to the viewer, **ustedes** to the audience. Never *vosotros* or
  *os*.
- **His words first.** If his transcripts use a word for a garment, use
  his: *camiseta*, *chaqueta*, *cinto* or *cinturón*, *fajar*,
  *atuendo*, *outfit*, *look*. Check with
  `grep -il "<word>" $GUSTO_HOME/transcripts/*.txt`.
- **Then Mexican usage**, understood across Latin America: *saco* (blazer),
  *chamarra* (casual jacket), *playera* (also fine), *tenis*, *pantalón de
  vestir*, *mezclilla*, *traje*, *celular*, *lentes*, *agarrar*, *platicar*.
- **No Spain-only words:** *vale*, *guay*, *tío*, *coger*, *americana*,
  *cazadora*, *zapatillas* (for sneakers), *móvil*, *gafas*, *vaqueros*,
  *ordenador*.
- **No heavy slang**, the kind that doesn't travel or doesn't fit the
  tone: *wey*, *chido*, *neta*, *chamba*, *padrísimo* in every line. One
  warm Mexicanism now and then is fine.
- **English fashion words** he already uses (*outfit*, *look*, *overshirt*,
  *chelsea*) stay. Gloss a new one the first time: "un *overshirt*, una
  sobrecamisa".
- **Numbers as spoken:** "200 pesos", "dos a cuatro centímetros", "a los
  cuarenta". Prices in Mexican pesos.

## Claims

- Style claims come from his rules and sources: "la regla de oro", "según
  GQ". No invented percentages. The "80%" in [ajuste] is his rule's own
  wording, so it may be said as *his* rule, not as a statistic.
- Body claims are said exactly as narrow as their study. "Un estudio con
  hombres de más de 60 años encontró…", not "la ciencia dice que…". The
  wording matches the row in `## Evidencia científica`.
